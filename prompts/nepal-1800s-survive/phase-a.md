# Phase A: Director's Proposal

## Header

**1. Title:** *Could You Survive 1814 Nepal?*
(Reference-language title: same. The narration is in English and you're working in English, so there's no separate translation. A Nepali reference translation can be added if you want one.)

**2. Core message:** Hill-village life 200 years ago was not "simple and peaceful". It was relentless physical work with no doctor, no clock and no safety net except other people. They lacked nearly everything we have, but they had each other.
**Opening hook:** *"You wake before dawn. No light switch. No clock. No doctor."* A modern viewer is dropped into 1814 with nothing.

**3. Setup:** `16:9` · `3 min = 18 clips × ~10 s` · **Style 4 — Cozy Storytelling** (full-color 2D cartoon environments, cinematic shot changes, a new setting for each segment)

**4. Narrator: complete voice profile (one voice for the whole video).** An elderly man of about seventy, off-screen, like a grandfather telling a story by the fire. Clear English with a light, gentle Nepali accent. Warm, deep, slightly weathered timbre. Low pitch, rich chest resonance, a soft touch of gravel, no breathiness. Slow, unhurried storytelling delivery with gentle pauses and restrained pitch variation, at a steady pace. Close, dry studio recording with no room echo. The emotion lives in the music and visuals; the voice never changes.
- **English word count:** 379 words (~21 per clip)
- **Estimated duration:** ~3:00 at about 2.1 words per second
- ⚠️ This is slightly brisk for a slow elderly voice. If generated clips sound rushed, I'll trim lines rather than speed up the narrator.

**5. Palette and environment mood (plain words):**

| Color mood | Meaning | Clips |
|---|---|---|
| Cool dawn blue-gray | Waking, the unknown | 1–2 |
| Fresh terrace green and millet gold | Labor, then reward | 3–5, 12 |
| Heavy slate gray and rain-dark brown | Danger, illness | 7–8 |
| Warm firelight amber | Home, healing, community | 9, 14, 16, 18 |
| Snow-peak white and thin-air sky blue | Distance, the journey, reflection | 11, 13 |

The protagonist's **rust-red sash** is the one recurring signature color in every clip.

**6. Music, tone and arc:**
- **Background music:** one gentle Nepali folk motif on sarangi (bowed fiddle), soft bansuri flute and a light madal hand-drum pulse. It keeps the same tempo, key and instruments from clip 1 to clip 18. It thins to a lone sarangi in clips 7–8, swells warmly in clips 12 and 15–16, and resolves softly in clip 18.
- **Tone:** warm but honest, bittersweet.
- **Arc:** curiosity → "it's simple" shattered → hidden hardship (food, monsoon, fever, war) → the hard truth (everything earned by the body, people as the safety net) → the debate.

## Storyboard (18 × ~10 s)

Cast in this video: **Farmer** (the protagonist, "you"), **Healer** (in clips 9, 10 and 16 only) and **Oxen** (clip 3 only). There are no other people.
↺ marks an approved deliberate return to an earlier location.
Overall screen direction runs **left → right** for journeys.

### Clip 1 · 0:00–0:10 · Golden Hook
**Location:** Farmhouse interior, pre-dawn. Low wooden beams, an earthen hearth with glowing embers, a straw mat, a tiny wooden window showing stars over dark peaks.

**Beats:**
- `[0–3s]` Extreme close-up of embers. The Farmer's face rises into frame as he sits up on the mat. His eyes open.
- `[3–7s]` He reaches toward the wall as if for a switch, and an icon-only lightbulb outline flickers and fades. He kneels and blows the embers into a small flame, which lights his face amber.
- `[7–10s]` A slow push-in past his shoulder to the window and the starry peaks. He stands and pushes the wooden door open, and blue dawn light floods in from the right.

**Visual devices:** close-up reveal, fading icon metaphor, a light change from dark to amber, a camera push, a door-light wipe.
**Audio:** The sarangi motif begins softly. Ember crackle, a distant rooster.
**Hands off to clip 2:** He steps out through the door, moving right, into blue dawn.

### Clip 2 · 0:10–0:20 · Disrupting Assumptions
**Location:** A stone village spring on the hillside at dawn. Mist in the valley, a moss-edged stone spout, a pine at the frame edge.

**Beats:**
- `[0–3s]` Wide shot: the Farmer walks in from the left carrying an empty round metal water pot. Mist drifts and birds cross.
- `[3–7s]` Medium shot: he holds the pot under the spout, and water splashes and sparkles. Icon-only sun and leaf symbols float gently, illustrating the "peaceful" idea.
- `[7–10s]` He heaves the full pot onto his shoulder with a visible strain and starts up a steep stone path to the right. The gentle icons dissolve.

**Visual devices:** lateral tracking, water sparkle, idealized icon metaphor, an effort pose, a steep-slope composition.
**Audio:** The motif continues. Trickling water, birdsong.
**Hands off to clip 3:** He climbs right, and the sun breaks over the peaks.

**Hands off to clip 4:** The ox's foreground wipe carries into the next shot.

## Facts used (check before Phase B)

- The Anglo-Nepal War began in **1814**.
- Hill soldiers were drawn from village men (narrated only as "may be called").
- Terraced rice farming, plowing with oxen, and stone-and-mud thatched houses.
- Grain ground on a hand-turned stone mill (jaanto).
- Loads carried in a doko basket with a namlo forehead strap.
- The khukuri as an everyday tool.
- Monsoon damage to terraces.
- The dhami/jhankri healer using drum, ritual and herbs.
- Healing knowledge passed on by word of mouth.
- Grain traded for Tibetan salt.
- Bamboo festival swings (Dashain) and oil lamps (Tihar).

The VO makes **no statistics, quotations or exact durations**. "Much of your day" and "for days" are deliberate approximations.
